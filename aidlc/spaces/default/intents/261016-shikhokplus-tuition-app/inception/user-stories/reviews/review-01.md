### Summary

The draft is well-constructed where it was built cleanly — the Given/When/Then discipline, the FR5/FR6.8 ledger-amendment consistency (AC6.3.3's charged-vs-claimed disambiguation correctly resolves the quality agent's sharpest objection, and AC5.1.2's rewrite to concurrent generation correctly resolves its other sharpest objection), and the US10.1 adversarial-privacy addition are genuine strengths that show real mob engagement. But the integration of the three specialist contributions is uneven and the gaps cluster on exactly the items the specialists flagged as highest-stakes and non-negotiable: none of the five observable OTP rate-limit thresholds reads as a testable criterion with a stated user-visible outcome, two of the three money boundary cases the quality agent called "would not ship without" are still missing (approved-exceeds-claimed, zero/negative submitted amount), the authorization-class tagging on the new IDOR story was dropped during integration, the developer's reasoned US8 split and corrected dependency edges were not applied despite being settled rather than left open, FR3.1's session-materialization gap (and the FR11.4 concurrent-edit gap, directly rebutted by name) remain unresolved with no counter-argument recorded, and all of design's accessibility findings were silently omitted. Several of these are not "nice to have" polish items — they are the exact failure modes the affirmed team practices (datastore-backed rate limits, the error taxonomy, the money-module boundary cases, the IDOR probe suite) were written to prevent, and they were already named once by a specialist and not carried through. This is not ready for engineering to build from without a return trip to resolve at least the OTP thresholds, the two missing money boundaries, and the US8/FR3.1 dependency and coverage gaps.
