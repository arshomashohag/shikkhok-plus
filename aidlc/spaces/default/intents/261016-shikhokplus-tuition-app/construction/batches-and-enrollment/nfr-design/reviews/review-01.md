### Summary

The reliability, scalability, security, and performance mechanisms stand up to direct verification against real PostgreSQL and the actual codebase — the fold, the partial index, the rename-vs-drop-and-add test design, and the RLS-as-backstop claim are all correct, and the "no guardian rows in production" premise the fold's exactness depends on is independently confirmed true by the B3 checkpoint record. The blocking problem is `traceability.json`: four of its 36 rows cite design sections that do not contain the content the row claims, with field names in one row (NFR14.18) that don't even match the upstream rule they are meant to trace. That is a broken cross-reference, not a judgment call, and it recurs enough (4 of 36 rows, >2 Major) to block readiness on its own, separate from the two Minor documentation-rigor gaps (the "seven cases" miscount and the "fails closed" mechanism being named but not specified).
