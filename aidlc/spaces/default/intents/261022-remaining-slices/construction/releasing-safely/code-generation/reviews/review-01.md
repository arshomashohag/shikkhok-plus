### Summary

The deletion machinery itself — the scoping map, the transaction-ordered refusal, the two-path comparison, the OTP purpose fix, the cascade-contradiction invariant, and the policy-drift digest — is unusually rigorous and the self-corrections documented in `code-summary.md` §2 are genuine (verified against the actual files, not merely asserted). The disqualifying defect is narrower but structural: the governance manifest's `Disposition` field and the deletion code's `Scope` field can silently disagree for a `NOT_PERSONAL`-scoped table, nothing tests for that disagreement, and it has already produced a specific, provable false claim in the generated privacy policy — the opposite of the "the two cannot drift apart" property this whole unit exists to establish. Given this is the terminal, release-blocking unit for a children's-data product about to be submitted for Play review, a regulator-facing document asserting the wrong thing about which identifiers get deleted is a release blocker in its own right, independent of how well everything else in the unit was built.
